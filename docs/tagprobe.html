Tag & probe — the idea, and the fine print

Ask one counter, grade the other. XCET040 and XCET043 are the two upstream threshold gas Cherenkov counters (~3 m of CO₂ each, PMT readout; amplitudes below are in ADC-eq, where 1 ADC-eq ≈ 0.244 mV). To measure what XCET43 does to electrons without biasing the answer, never select on XCET43 — select on XCET40 (“the tag”) and then look at XCET43 (“the probe”) with no cut at all: zeros included. Because the tag decision uses different photons, in a different gas volume, seen by a different PMT, the probe’s spectrum is an unbiased electron response. Every headline XCET number — both pe/bar slopes, the efficiency panel, and the 1-pe rulers themselves (a ruler = A1pe, the measured single-photoelectron amplitude that converts ADC-eq to photoelectrons) — rests on this construction.

The fine print is three assumptions: (1) the tag really selected the species you think (purity); (2) tag and probe are statistically independent given the particle (no per-event correlation); (3) the tagged particle actually traversed the probe (matched acceptance). None of these may be assumed. Below, each one is measured — on this campaign’s own 18 calibration runs (macros/XCETTagProbe.C, Output/summary/XCETTagProbe_summary.txt). Where this page says “the review board”, it means the internal adversarial review of the XCET calibration (campaign log) whose open questions this note answers.

Five ways tag & probe can lie — and what each one measured

1 · Species impurity. The known case is run 34: a 1.3 bar tag at +5 GeV radiates on pions (threshold ~0.86 bar) and poisons the probe — excluded, and visible in the calibration tables (its probe mean is 2.6× low at matched pressure). The subtler case is energy: an electron’s Cherenkov yield saturates in β, but not instantly — at 0.06 bar the electron threshold is ~70 MeV and the yield reaches ~95% of saturation only above ~0.3 GeV (~0.1 GeV at 0.4 bar). Halo electrons above that pressure-dependent floor radiate like beam electrons and are valid calibration statistics — the same population as the 11 GeV trial’s outsized miss class (see the summary); softer ones form a yield mixture, flagged below as the bootstrap’s leading residual systematic.

2 · Fake tags. A dark photoelectron in the tag counter over a non-radiating hadron admits that hadron into the probe sample, where it reads zero. Measured off-time (dark pulses are uniform in the 205 ns window; beam signals sit at one sample position), with the in-window pileup component subtracted: above the calibration’s 40 ADC-eq tag threshold, XCET40’s dark occupancy is ~1.7% per full window — its 1-pe amplitude (40.2) sits exactly at that threshold, so a single dark photoelectron can fire the tag — falling to 0.5% above 60 and 0.03% above 100. XCET43’s is ~0.4% above 40 (a fake needs ~2–3 coincident dark pe) and <0.05% above 60. Both counters fake; the asymmetry is a factor ~4, and at low pressure — where real tags are scarce — the fakes become a double-digit fraction of the probe sample for both, worst for XCET43’s.

3 · Per-event correlation. The coincidence-efficiency product assumes independence. The Pearson correlation of the two amplitudes (both counters fired) runs r = +0.10 to +0.68 and varies between runs at identical settings (run 39: 0.15; run 40: 0.46) — intensity-linked. The threshold scan bounds tag-selection bias on the probe mean at ≤0.7% at 0.59 bar; the large mid-pressure correlations (r up to 0.68 near 0.44 bar, where tag purity is excellent) are not explained by the measured off-time pileup rate (~0.13%/event) alone and remain an open item — they matter for coincidence-product predictions more than for tag-and-probe means.

4 · Acceptance mismatch. A tagged particle that misses the probe fakes inefficiency. With full-window tags the empty-probe floor at 0.55–0.59 bar measures 0.03–0.2% (now tabulated in the summary); with time-matched tag and probe windows it collapses to ≤0.05% — most of even that small floor was out-of-time tags, not geometry. The counters really do see the same beam.

5 · The amplitude estimator itself. A minimum over 1024 samples has an extreme-value noise envelope: the 99th-percentile full-window “amplitude” of a genuinely empty channel runs up to ~56 ADC-eq (run- and channel-dependent: typically 45–56 for XCET40, 14–43 for XCET43), versus 5–16 in a 71-sample prompt window. Full-window amplitudes near threshold are noise-biased — the same lesson the module-side transfer calibration taught us, now quantified for the XCETs.

The smoking gun — why the low-pressure calibration points sag

The review board flagged XCET43’s calibration residuals as pressure-structured (χ²/ndf 79/17, a −0.37 pe floating intercept, a 30% split between tag-threshold groups). The threshold scan finds the mechanism. At 0.59 bar the probe mean is flat to ≤0.7% against tag hardness — pure sample, independent counters. At 0.09 bar the same scan climbs for both probes: +24% by 60 ADC-eq and +54% by 80 for XCET43’s, +20% by 60 for XCET40’s — raising the tag threshold purges fake-tagged hadrons out of diluted samples.

And the numbers close. The measured dark-only occupancies above the calibration’s 40 ADC-eq tag (1.7% per window for XCET40, 0.4% for XCET43), multiplied by each run’s non-radiating population, predict 15–40% fake dilution of XCET43’s probe and 4–14% of XCET40’s at 0.06–0.16 bar — consistent with the scan plateaus, run by run (the closure table is in the summary). So both counters’ low-pressure calibration points are biased low, XCET43’s ~3–4× worse: that is the shape of much of what the board flagged — the χ², the negative intercept, the threshold-group split, and most of the relative-ratio climb. Two things this mechanism does not explain: the 0.55–0.6 bar cluster sitting above the line (fakes only push down; open, pending the refit), and the mid-pressure amplitude correlations of failure mode 3. The identified fix — retag the calibration with prompt-window, fake-subtracted tags — is queued; it will move the low-pressure points up, by an amount the refit itself must quantify.

The counting bootstrap — a gain ruler with no resolved 1-pe peak required

What this does and does not settle. The board’s dominant concern was XCET43’s single-run, pedestal-edge ruler: if A1pe(43) were really ~25–26 rather than 21.8, the 26 pe/bar yield anomaly would evaporate. The bootstrap — with systematics independent of the pedestal edge — lands on 21.6 ± 2.3 ± 3.7: consistent with the ruler, and the anomaly-erasing value is disfavored at ~1σ but not excluded. The honest caveat cuts the other way too: the bootstrap’s own XCET40 control scatters beyond its errors in exactly the lowest-pressure regime (run 44 reads low), where sub-saturation-floor halo electrons (failure mode 1) form a yield mixture the model does not yet carry — a per-run composition systematic that caps how hard either counter’s number can be pushed. The corrections are not cosmetic: naive → robust moves individual runs by 40–160% (the naive column is kept in the summary table as forensics). Getting here took three honest failures, each of which became a measurement: the naive full-window version fails on the noise envelope (mode 5) and fake tags (mode 2); a quiet-counter dark control fails because at low pressure “quiet” events are dominated by real electrons whose probe Poisson-zeroed; an off-time control works once the in-window pileup component (common to both counters, measured by XCET43’s rate above 100 ADC-eq) is separated.

The recipe box — what we’d hand the next crew

1 · Tag in time, not in window. Requiring the tag in a prompt window cuts dark-fake probability by the window ratio (here 14×) and costs no real tags — beam particles are synchronous.
2 · Measure dark rates off-time, on all events — the only control sample free of beam, halo, and Poisson-zero contamination. Split off pileup using the second counter’s high-threshold rate (pileup is common mode; dark is not), then close the loop: dark rate × non-radiating population should reproduce the threshold-scan dilution, run by run. Ours does.
3 · Calibrate the acceptance floor at high pressure, where Poisson zeros are extinct: any prompt-empty probe there is geometry or readout. Ours: ≤0.05% with time-matched windows.
4 · Bootstrap the SPE gain from counting (zero fraction ⊗ mean, solved for λ and A1pe): works on any threshold-Cherenkov pair with waveform readout, needs no resolved 1-pe peak, and cross-checks the spectral ruler with largely independent systematics (the one imported spectral quantity is the relative 1-pe width). The ratio A1 = mean/λ is first-order robust against sample-composition biases that shift λ itself.
5 · Exploit β saturation — above its floor. Halo electrons comfortably above the counter’s own threshold energy (here ~0.3 GeV at the lowest pressures, ~0.1 GeV at 0.4 bar) are free calibration statistics; softer ones dilute the yield and belong in the systematics.
6 · Distrust full-window minima near threshold. The 1024-sample extreme-value envelope reads up to ~2.5 XCET43 photoelectrons on an empty channel.

The verdict — what this validation changes, and what it leaves standing

Standing, strengthened: the tag-and-probe construction itself (unbiased where pure; tag-selection bias ≤0.7% where tested); XCET40’s calibration end to end (ruler recovered by counting at ~0.5σ); and XCET43’s 1-pe ruler — the board’s dominant concern — now independently supported: 21.6 ± 2.3 ± 3.7 against the pedestal-edge 21.8, with the anomaly-erasing 25–26 disfavored at ~1σ. On present evidence the 26 pe/bar yield is real photons, and XCET43’s N0 ≈ 100/cm — the thin-window advantage of the 4.2-bar vessel — remains the favored reading, pending the refit below.

Changed: the “pressure-structured residuals” are no longer a mystery — they are fake-tag dilution of both counters’ low-pressure probes (XCET43’s ~3–4× worse), quantified by the threshold scan and closed against the measured dark rates, and they bias the fitted slopes low at the low-pressure end. The measured-efficiency points on the calibration figure are operational truths for the campaign’s actual tag definition; a future analysis wanting electron efficiency should use prompt tags. Queued: the prompt-tag, fake-subtracted refit of both slopes; a sub-saturation halo-mixture term in the bootstrap systematics (and an extension of the XCET43 bootstrap toward 0.15 bar); the unexplained mid-pressure amplitude correlations; the 0.55–0.6 bar high-side cluster; and the same treatment folded into the T10 instrumentation note.
