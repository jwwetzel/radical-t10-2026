What we tested

The RADiCAL module is a shashlik calorimeter — alternating tungsten and LYSO:Ce tile layers — sampled at shower maximum by an interchangeable 2×2 array of capillaries (14×14 mm face) that collect the tiles’ scintillation light into dual-gain SiPMs and a CAEN DT5742 (DRS4, 5 GS/s). A micro-channel plate upstream provides the ~10 ps time reference; two threshold Cherenkov counters tag electrons in hardware at every energy. The tungsten/LYSO:Ce stack is identical in every configuration; between campaigns only the capillary set was swapped — so every difference between the three datasets is the light-collection channel, not the calorimeter.

Reference-free timing — Randy’s diagonal difference

Every published σ_t includes the MCP+DRS reference jitter. The diagonal-pair estimator — average capillaries NW+SE and NE+SW separately, subtract — cancels the reference event-by-event; for independent capillaries, σ(diff)/2 is the intrinsic 4-capillary resolution. Applied to all 18 scan runs:

Validation: for LuAG the implied reference jitter comes out at 107–114 ps, energy-independent from 5–9 GeV — matching the independently measured ~105 ps DRS inter-group + MCP budget. The method works. And it found something: for DSB1 at 5–7 GeV, σ(diff)/2 slightly exceeds the reference-included width — impossible for independent capillaries — revealing correlated per-capillary timing terms (shared shower fluctuations / position quadrupole) that the 4-capillary mean averages down but the diagonal difference does not. For the high-light channel the diagonal estimate is therefore an upper bound (†same flag at EJ199’s 9 GeV point); EJ199 columns use containment floors matched to its scan (re-derived after an audit caught a floor mismatch); subtracting the validated 110 ps reference instead gives DSB1 intrinsic estimates of ~145 ps at 5 GeV, ~137 ps at 7 GeV, and ~75 ps at 9 GeV (from the 133 ps median) — the last to be confirmed with the downstream reference planned for the next beam test, exactly the “rich man’s version” of this estimator.

§Units caveat, stated plainly: ADC-eq is a readout-relative unit, and the MIP MPVs carry estimator and beam-species systematics (see the LuAG linearity forensics) — both MIP sets here are same-beam +5 GeV runs, so the ratio is meaningful even where the absolute scale is soft. With the LYSO:Ce tiles fixed, these ratios measure the capillaries as light-collection channels: the DSB:Ce channel captures/converts the tile light ~2.2× more efficiently per shower GeV than LuAG while collecting less per crossing MIP — a ~3.5× difference in response-per-MIP between the two channels. An absolute photoelectron scale (dark-pulse calibration) is possible from existing data and planned.

†The +5 GeV fraction depends on the collimation configuration: 9.1% with the early tight-acceptance settings (run 15), 5.7% with acceptance collimators open (run 37) — composition is not a constant of the momentum setting alone. ‡At −11 GeV the published composition has e⁻→0, yet our contained tags measure as hard electrons (E-eq ≈ 10 GeV; see the 11 GeV trial below) — evidence the tables are again pessimistic at high |p|; the tag’s halo component remains uncertain, so we quote a tag rate. Above ~5 GeV we otherwise consistently find more electrons than the published tables — reproducible day-to-day at the few-percent level across two modules. Other beam findings: MCP pulse height is species-correlated (electrons pulse smaller — a high trigger threshold silently rejects them); ~48 triggers/spill at 1 GeV with run-rate differences driven by supercycle cadence, not intensity; and XCET radiator gas at low-energy pressures is beamline material the machine bookkeeping assumes away — 11 bar of CO₂ is ~17% X₀ and visibly widens the beam. A 1.5 bar setting at +5 GeV radiates on pions too: coincidence 69.7% = the beam’s combined e+μ+π content, measured to a percent.

Conclusions so far

Shower-max sampling times electromagnetic showers at the hundred-picosecond scale from a 14 mm module. DSB1 reaches 133 ± 5 ps at 9 GeV with all reference jitter included, and its shower-time resolution beats LuAG’s at every energy — traced mechanistically to its ~3× shorter scintillation tail — and over 1–9 GeV its trend is pure photostatistics, (361 ± 4)/√E ps, with the constant term unresolved (the earlier “79 ps” figure was a two-point-anchor artifact and is retired). Material choice is now a measured trade: DSB:Ce buys ~2.2× the light per GeV and the faster pulse; LuAG:Ce buys more signal per crossing MIP. And the campaign returned beam knowledge T10 didn’t have: in-situ electron fractions above the published tables at 5–9 GeV/c, their dependence on collimation, and the XCET radiator gas as unbudgeted beamline material at low momentum.

Next

Confirm the DSB1 intrinsic timing (~75 ps-class at 9 GeV) with an independent downstream reference; irradiation campaign on DSB:Ce and LuAG:Ce capillary assemblies (longest-lead item — it decides the down-select); SPS beam test at 20–150 GeV with tracker, pulser + temperature logging, and a 3×3 matrix; dark-pulse photoelectron calibration; connection of all three capillary types to the high-energy datasets from earlier campaigns (pending data access).
