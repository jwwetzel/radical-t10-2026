## Lens 4 first — Committee chair's report (verbatim)

VERDICT

The committee's judgment is split, and deliberately so. For the muon collider: yes — this concept should be selected for continued R&D. The requirement there is cell-level background rejection at 30–60 ps on few-to-tens-of-GeV EM deposits in an extreme radiation field, and the DSB:Ce capillary channel's photostatistics-limited scaling with no resolved constant term, at a cell size already matched to ECAL granularity, is a credible trajectory toward that spec — the current 2–4x shortfall at few-GeV deposits is a stated R&D target, not a disqualifier. For FCC: a qualified yes, but only as reframed. As a high-energy EM shower time-stamp (FCC-hh) or an embedded timing/position layer (FCC-ee), the extrapolated 25–35 ps at 50–100 GeV is competitive with CMS-ECAL-class precedent — if the constant term cooperates, which is unmeasured. As a calorimeter, and as a 5–10 ps vertexing device, it is not selectable on this evidence. Separately: the concept earns the next tranche; this prototype is a TRL 3–4 validation of a light-collection channel, not a system demonstration, and several of its headline numbers must be re-derived before any external review.

WHY

Five strengths were decisive. First, the timing result itself: DSB:Ce at 133 ± 5 ps at 9 GeV reference-included, with a median-consistent intrinsic ladder of roughly 111/87/75 ps at 5/7/9 GeV compatible with pure photostatistics. Second, the constant term was shown to be engineerable, not module-intrinsic — swapping only the capillary set at fixed W/LYSO stack halved it. Third, error accounting rare at this readiness level: the diagonal-difference estimator independently recovered the known ~110 ps reference floor and, in failing on the bright channel, discovered correlated capillary timing — the physics floor for multi-fiber averaging, found before a tower was ever built. Fourth, exemplary systematics forensics: the saturation prosecution, the gain-ladder diagnosis, the invalidation of the team's own MIP calibration anchor, and the conversion of the EJ-199 failure into a quantified contaminant fingerprint. Fifth, the demonstrated bottleneck is the lab DAQ, not the detector.

Five gaps were equally decisive. The flagship 75 ps is a quadrature subtraction of near-equal numbers below the DAQ's own jitter floor, with the reference validated only on LuAG runs before an MCP voltage change; relatedly, the quoted 79 ps constant term sits below the validated 107–114 ps reference and is unphysical — it must not be quoted again, and the published intrinsic ladder mixes estimators. Radiation hardness — the project's raison d'être — was not measured, and the down-select is inverted: the timing winner is the least radiation-qualified material in the stack. Intrinsic energy resolution was never measured at any point; every σ/E is position-smearing dominated, linearity holds only to a ±15 % gain envelope with no monitoring plane, and the calorimeter case is simply unmade (honestly so — the site never overclaims). Dynamic range already fails at 11 GeV while target showers are 30–100x brighter, and 9 GeV is the maximum clean energy, leaving the decision-critical constant term with no lever arm. Finally, the record itself: registry entries contradicting DAQ metadata, merged input files with no generating code, a containment-floor inconsistency that changes the published EJ-199 intrinsic numbers, and stale figures — reproducibility needs a cleanup pass.

OPEN QUESTIONS

Ranked: (1) Does 75 ps at 9 GeV survive an independent downstream reference of ≤20–30 ps jitter on a non-DRS4 path, and does the LuAG-validated common mode apply to the DSB1 runs? (2) What is the intrinsic constant term at 20–150 GeV, measured directly at SPS rather than by subtraction — is it ~20 ps or ~60 ps? (3) Does DSB:Ce retain light yield and timing after MGy-class gamma and 10¹⁵–10¹⁶ neq/cm², and does LuAG overtake it post-irradiation? (4) Is the correlated capillary timing physical shower fluctuation or common-mode readout — separable with a downstream reference, shuffled cabling, and an impact-position map? (5) What is intrinsic σ/E at 1–9 GeV with impact-position tagging, decomposed against an absolute photoelectron scale? (6) What is the event-by-event longitudinal-fluctuation term of shower-max-only sampling, from simulation validated against the measured Longo migration? (7) What pixel and dynamic-range budget delivers linearity at 30–100x today's light?

THE ROAD

First, extract what this dataset still holds: re-derive the intrinsic ladder with one estimator and retire the 79 ps figure; fix the DiagDiff EJ-199 floors and re-log those points; replace two-point trend solves with weighted fits; compare LG-path pulse shapes across channels to settle whether tile light is actually being collected; reconcile registry against FINDINGS, add checksums and provenance for the derived files, and regenerate the two pre-house-style figures before anything is shown externally. Second, bench work: the dark-pulse photoelectron calibration, per-batch incoming-material QA (absorption/emission plus decay-time fingerprint), and a dynamic-range design note identifying the front-end ASIC and clock-distribution path. Third, launch the irradiation campaign now, on real capillary assemblies of both DSB:Ce and LuAG:Ce including couplings and SiPMs — it is the longest-lead item and it, not beam data, decides the down-select. Fourth, the next beam test: SPS H2/H4 at 20–150 GeV, an independent downstream time reference, a tracker or hodoscope, LED/laser pulser with temperature logging and frozen bias, a 3x3 module matrix, rescoped low-gain range, and lead-converter runs for e/π versus energy. Passing that gate would make this a rare energy-plus-timing device; failing it still leaves a fundable shower-max timing layer.

(2) SPS run (H2/H4, 10-100 GeV) with rescoped LG dynamic range and a 3x3 module matrix: deliverable = stochastic and constant terms in the FCC-ee-relevant regime, lateral containment/sharing, e/pi rejection vs E with converter control runs, plus the downstream time reference to confirm the ~75 ps intrinsic timing point. ### Detector-systems / engineering readiness reviewer (practicality, scalability, QA, DAQ)

Engineering maturity verdict: this campaign is a well-executed TRL 3-4 physics validation of the light-collection channel (proof of concept in a relevant beam environment), not a system demonstration. The physics case that emerged — the DSB:Ce channel's ~145/137 ps intrinsic at 5/7 GeV and ~75 ps (to confirm) at 9 GeV, with the ref-included constant term nearly halved vs LuAG (323/sqrt(E)+79 vs 389/sqrt(E)+138 ps; FINDINGS.md lines 561-563, 659-670) — is exactly what justifies the next engineering phase. But every system-level axis I am asked to judge is either untested or was found wanting during the campaign itself, and — critically — the caveats are honestly documented in /Users/jameswetzel/Dropbox/01_Research/HEP/RADiCAL/02_Analysis/2026-08_CERN_T10/RADiCAL_TB_T10_Aug-2026/Output/run_12/FINDINGS.md, so my job here is largely to price them, not discover them.

(1) RADIATION HARDNESS — critical, and NOT touched by this campaign. RADiCAL's raison d'etre is rad-hard EM calorimetry at FCC-hh/muon-collider doses (order MGy ionizing and 1e15-1e16 1-MeV-neq/cm^2 at shower max over lifetime). Literature ranking of the four materials, which this campaign's channel selection must be checked against: LYSO:Ce (the fixed tiles) is the best-qualified — gamma-hard to ~1 MGy with small induced absorption and hadron-qualified by CMS MTD to ~2e14 neq/cm^2; beyond ~1e15 neq the data thins even for LYSO. LuAG:Ce (garnet crystal fiber) has a solid pedigree from fiber-calorimeter R&D (LHCb SPACAL-type studies), gamma-hard to ~1 MGy-class doses. DSB:Ce glass — the campaign's timing WINNER — has by far the thinnest radiation literature: Ce-doped silicate glasses historically darken at doses where garnets are fine, published DSB:Ce hardness data extend only to tens-of-kGy-class exposures, and dose-rate/recovery behavior is essentially uncharacterized. EJ-199 plastic WLS: plastics degrade at 0.01-0.1 MGy; a plastic WLS at shower max in an FCC-hh detector is a non-starter without new chemistry, independent of the contaminant episode. The uncomfortable engineering conclusion: the channel that won on timing is the one least qualified to survive the environment the project exists for. An irradiation campaign of actual capillary ASSEMBLIES (fiber + capillary + coupling/glue + SiPM) is the single most important critical-path item, and it gates the DSB-vs-LuAG down-select — a post-irradiation LuAG that keeps 180 ps may beat a darkened DSB.

(2) SiPM OPERATION — major, demonstrated in-campaign. The cross-run gain ladder (+/-15%, consistent with a few degC hall swing at ~5%/degC; FINDINGS.md lines 488-495), the proof that MIP normalization is NOT a valid cross-run gain monitor (species + noise-estimator bias, lines 479-487), the mid-campaign +1 V bias change that reset the calibration reference (lines 210-214), and the untaped light leak doubling daytime noise via DC photocurrent (lines 388-403) together demonstrate that the system as operated has NO gain-monitoring plane at all. This is a solved problem in the field (per-channel temperature readback + bias compensation, plus LED/laser pulser injection — the log itself prescribes exactly this at line 493-494), but it must be designed in: pulser light distribution to every channel is a real mechanical/optical design constraint at 1e5-1e6 channels, not an afterthought. Until it exists, response linearity claims are capped at the +/-15% envelope — which the site correctly states (index.html, Materials card). Additionally, HG baseline RMS tracking bias raises (24-38 -> 81-111 ADC-eq, lines 252-254) previews the post-irradiation reality: SiPM dark currents grow orders of magnitude at 1e13-1e14 neq, so the real system needs active cooling and an annealing strategy for the photodetector plane — nowhere yet in the design.

(3) DYNAMIC RANGE — major, already exhausted at test-beam energies. The brightest channel (DSB1, ~1,350 ADC-eq/GeV) soft-hit the ~13.6k ADC-eq LG ceiling at 11 GeV (lines 552-556); the ceiling therefore sits at roughly a 10 GeV full-response equivalent. FCC-hh EM showers are 50 GeV to multi-TeV: the front end needs order x30-x100 more range. The dual-gain architecture is the right shape, but note the HG chain clipped 1-12% of pulses at FIVE GeV before offset retuning (lines 25-31), and the campaign spent real effort on clip-recovery analysis heroics (srCFD, wall-aware transfer fits, lines 232-243) — acceptable R&D triage, unacceptable as a design pattern. Scaling also re-opens SiPM pixel saturation, acquitted at these light levels (lines 475-477) but not at x30 photons — and the pixel budget cannot even be computed yet because the absolute photoelectron scale is only 'planned' (index.html Materials card). Separately, the 1024-sample record truncates the slow LG component at ~50% of peak (lines 54-59): integration-window design is currently mismatched to the LYSO decay.

(4) CHANNEL COUNT / COST — unquantified. 4 capillaries x dual gain = 8 waveform channels per 14x14 mm module face; any physical calorimeter is 1e5-1e6 channels. No capillary manufacturing yield, unit cost, or assembly-time data exist from this campaign. One genuinely useful NEW input: the correlated per-capillary timing discovered in DSB1 (sigma(diff)/2 exceeding the ref-included width, lines 663-667) means the 4-capillary timing gain does NOT scale as sqrt(N) in high-light channels — which argues against over-provisioning per-module timing channels and is directly relevant to the cost model, but first the correlation must be shown to be physical (shower fluctuations/position quadrupole) rather than a common-mode readout artifact.

(5) MANUFACTURING QA — the EJ-199 episode is a textbook supply-chain lesson. A spec-compliant EJ-199 (tuned for LuO:Yb, absorption excluding LYSO's 425 nm) should have been nearly blind; observed ~550-650 ADC-eq/GeV with a 6.5 ns tail is in-beam, quantified support for the Eljen-contaminant hypothesis (lines 636-641, 652-657). A beam test was used as an expensive spectrometer to discover an incoming-materials defect. For production: mandatory per-batch bench QA (absorption/emission spectra, decay-time fingerprint — the 12.5/6.5/4.9 ns tau table is exactly the right observable) before anything ships to a beam or a detector. Credit where due: the team converted the failure into a quantified fingerprint for the vendor conversation.

(6) DAQ — DRS4 is a lab instrument, not a scalable architecture, and it also set the measurement floor. The CAEN DT5742's inter-group jitter of 104.5-115 ps (lines 84, 223, 660-661) is COMPARABLE TO OR LARGER THAN the DSB1 intrinsic being claimed (~75 ps at 9 GeV): the campaign's headline number is a reference-subtracted inference sitting under the electronics floor of its own DAQ, which is why the log itself flags it 'to be confirmed with a downstream reference next beam test' (line 669). Switched-capacitor waveform digitizers have ms-scale dead time and kHz-class burst rates — fine at T10's 2-30 Hz, irrelevant at 40 MHz BX. The path to scale is a fast-timing front-end ASIC (HGCROC/ETROC/FastIC-class TOA+TOT or on-chip CFD) with <=30-50 ps electronics jitter, plus a clock-distribution system at the 10-20 ps level — without which the demonstrated sub-100 ps intrinsic constant term is unrecoverable at system level. Neither exists in the project plan as evidenced here.

Also structural, already documented but with engineering consequences: no tracker (sigma/E position-smearing dominated everywhere, intrinsic ~10% bounded exactly once at 5 GeV, lines 102-109), single module (no lateral containment, no inter-module cross-talk or dead-material data), shower-max-only sampling (this is a timing/position channel qualification, not a calorimeter qualification), and T10's 11 GeV ceiling with the standing purity caveat (open markers, lines 595-608) — the energies where the dynamic-range and saturation questions actually live require SPS (H2/H4) beams.
